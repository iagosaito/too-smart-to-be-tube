How it works

  1. 1

    Add it to Chrome

    A Categories section appears in YouTube's sidebar, right below Subscriptions.

  2. 2

    Sort your channels

    Create categories and move channels into them, one by one or many at once.

  3. 3

    Watch by category

    Click a category to get a feed of just those channels: the latest videos and Shorts.

What you can do

  • Unlimited categories

    Create as many as you need and rename them in place. No caps, no Pro plan.

  • A feed per category

    The newest videos from just those channels, in YouTube's own grid.

  • Shorts, too

    Vertical Shorts from the same channels, interleaved so no one floods the page.

  • All your subscriptions

    Reads your full list, not just the few channels in the sidebar, and keeps it in sync.

  • Sort by latest upload

    See who posted "2 days ago" and who has been quiet for a year.

  • CSV import & export

    Categorize hundreds of channels in a spreadsheet. Review every change, and undo it.

  • Looks like YouTube

    Follows YouTube's light and dark themes and its language: English, Português, Español.

  • Keyboard friendly

    Real buttons and menus, arrow-key navigation and screen-reader labels.

Add it to Chrome

Coming soon to the Chrome Web Store. Until then, install it from source in about a minute:

Terminal
$ git clone https://github.com/iagosaito/too-smart-to-be-tube.git
$ cd too-smart-to-be-tube && npm ci && npm run build
  1. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  2. Click Load unpacked and choose the project folder.
  3. Open YouTube and click Categories in the sidebar.

Built for Chrome. Other Chromium browsers (Edge, Brave, Opera) should work too.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No premium tier, no trial, no ads. It's an open-source project: if it helps you, a star on GitHub is the only payment.

Can it get my YouTube account in trouble?

It doesn't change anything on your account: no unsubscribing, no likes, no automation. Videos come from the subscriptions page you'd load anyway; Shorts and older uploads come from public channel pages, requested without your cookies, slowly, and cached.

Where are my categories stored? What if I switch computers?

In your browser's local extension storage, and nowhere else. To move them, export a CSV and import it on the other machine.

I follow 500+ channels. Will it be slow?

No. Rendering, search and sorting are built for hundreds of channels, and category feeds load in small batches you control.

YouTube changed something and it broke. What now?

Please open an issue on GitHub. The extension is checked against the real youtube.com, so fixes are usually quick.
